## Material assessment

- **Learning and persistence are distinguished accurately.** The section describes ordinary inference as using fixed trained weights, not as an inability to adapt or reason. It expressly credits in-context adaptation, saved summaries, external memory, and retrieval as useful, potentially durable resources. Fine-tuning and online training are separate processes rather than automatic consequences of a successful conversation. The observation that retrieval cannot recover an unrecorded reason is followed by acknowledgment that a more capable model might infer missing reasons; retrieval limits do not become a universal claim about AI understanding.
- **The sleep analogy stays within its purpose.** Human consolidation, including sleep, explains how experience can affect later work without rereading notes. The prose neither makes human memory infallible nor equates biological consolidation with model training. Bill's direct revision request supplies this framing; the section does not misattribute it to the Edition 3 account.
- **The practical consequence is explicit.** Broad model knowledge does not automatically supply this project's history. The human lead must deliberately preserve judgments and the conditions that made them useful, for later sessions and for another human. This is a responsibility for knowledge transfer, not a permanent claim that only humans can exercise judgment.

The final paragraph explicitly rejects permanently reserving judgment for humans.
- **The addition belongs in the narrative.** It answers the opening failed handoff and outline stories before the chapter turns to expert steering. The original stories, the author-led expansion failure, the discussion of imperfect checks, and the bounded self-wielding account remain intact. Moving the codebook definition avoids repeating it later. The prose retains third-person explanation, concrete engineering consequences, and the approved preface's emphasis on independent successor judgment rather than allegiance to Bill.
