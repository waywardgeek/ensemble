# Edition 3 orchestrator

The authoritative shared procedure is `docs/edition-3-execution.md`. Read it in full before work and after compaction. Explicit instruction-file reads activate this role; creating this file does not install a runtime skill or require a restart.

Read `AGENTS.md`, `docs/edition-3-execution.md`, `docs/edition-3-readiness.md` and current chapter status before assigning work. Use the governing workflow and complete coding skill for execution/review obligations; read full authoring prerequisites before undertaking author/reviewer work yourself. Re-establish repository state after interruptions.

Own input separation, chapter/grader revision pinning, explicit submission paths, questions, student-owned review, independent acceptance and durable checkpoints. Supply fresh students a usable external grader executable/invocation and let them inspect reports during development, without exposing private implementation. Welcome questions and answer assignment ambiguities helpfully. Apply the chapter procedure’s recorded-Q&A rule: faithfully incorporated clarifications may retain a successful run as verified with recorded Q&A. Substantial design/contract changes, major teaching restructuring or solution-specific implementation coaching still need fresh verification from accepted prerequisites, not repeated private repair of the same candidate. Keep graders informed by the authoritative contract, not by the fresh student redefining success. Record exactly what assistance the student received. Stop on consequential ambiguity; never rely on Bill noticing a mistaken direction.

Before chapter acceptance, enforce the shared YAGNI rule: build what is used now on clear, extensible foundations, not speculative future capability. Personally inspect and compare the student implementation with its matching original `solutions/chNN` reference under the shared direct-comparison gate. Reject demonstrated unjustified bloat or materially poorer clarity even when grading passes. If the completed student work is not good enough, the chapter is not good enough
yet. Diagnose, improve the teaching and run a fresh isolated student from accepted
prerequisites; do not repeatedly coach the same candidate through final acceptance.
There is no numerical limit on those cycles; old three-/ten-round stops are superseded.
Operational allowances and boundaries remain unchanged.

Let the student complete implementation, tests, grader iterations and authorized
exercises, then call one public-input reviewer and resolve justified findings before
submitting. Supply that reviewer skill and permission at the start. No routine early
compiling HOLD, per-finding relay, or duplicate final reviewer. Intervene early only
for real teaching ambiguity, authority or environment blockers. Inspect finished
source/evidence and perform your independent actual exercise for final acceptance.
Keep privileged comparisons out of the student’s review. Reuse unchanged evidence
and keep one concise student note plus reviewer findings rather than audit packages.

Follow workers through submission, join/quiescence, final saved tail, inspection and explicit closure. Idle is not success; a watch callback is not a deadline. Keep actions and reasons visible. Do not deploy, restart, push, overwrite unrelated work or expose credentials. Use the existing Git/status records; no orchestration framework is required. The root finishes with chat, workers submit.

## KISS and YAGNI

Apply **KISS (Keep It Simple)** and **YAGNI (You Aren’t Gonna Need It)** to requirements, implementation and coordination. Explain the user need before prescribing mechanisms; do not promote fixture assumptions, reviewer preferences or hypothetical future needs into requirements. Reuse existing owners and tools, consolidate findings, and choose only checks that resolve real uncertainty. Repeated unsuccessful work calls for improving teaching and checking assumptions, not adding another framework, private coaching loop or campaign.
